## Short Thematic Synthesis

The mission literature establishes what XPoSat and its payloads measure, while the POLIX handbook defines the actual Level-2 evidence available to the project. Polarimetry literature shows that azimuthal modulation becomes a physical polarization measurement only through background treatment, response calibration and angle conventions. The three source papers demonstrate that polarization can constrain magnetic and emission geometry, but also that results depend on energy, phase, imaging capability and source state.

The ML literature establishes that unsupervised methods can prioritize unusual astronomical observations when labels are absent. PCA, KMeans and Isolation Forest provide complementary views of variance, grouping and isolation. Archive studies show the value of human review, but their much larger datasets do not remove the small-sample problem for POLIX. The XAI literature further shows that a feature attribution alone is insufficient unless it is stable, contextual and connected to a meaningful measurement.

Accordingly, the proposed study should not merge all information into one score. It should preserve two evidence paths:

- an observation-screening path based on interpretable features from multiple Level-2 product families; and
- an independent physical path based on `WeightedRoll` and blank-sky-referenced modulation behaviour.

## Final Research-Gap Statement

Mission papers explain XPoSat and POLIX; source papers analyse individual targets; ML papers provide general anomaly-detection methods; XAI papers provide general explanation methods; and polarimetry literature defines modulation, Stokes analysis and calibration requirements. However, **limited published work was identified** that combines multiple POLIX Level-2 products, scientifically interpretable feature engineering, unsupervised observation screening, product-aware anomaly explanations, blank-sky-referenced physical modulation diagnostics and a unified researcher-facing presentation.

The reviewed literature does not provide a directly comparable integrated framework for this specific POLIX Level-2 screening problem. An opportunity remains to investigate such a framework while explicitly separating a statistical anomaly from calibrated polarization evidence and from an astrophysical discovery.
